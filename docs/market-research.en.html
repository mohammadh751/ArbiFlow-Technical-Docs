Exchange research before integration

A meaningful part of ArbiFlow development involved understanding the documented and observed behavior of three exchanges—because one acquisition pattern does not fit every market.

Three venues, different acquisition patterns

WALLEX

Multiple data layers

Historical research showed that price/overview feeds and full depth differ in both volume and information semantics. Discovery and precise verification should be treated separately.

BITPIN

Light discovery, selective depth

Lightweight public data can support initial discovery, while detailed depth can be reserved for markets that warrant closer review.

NOBITEX

Overview is not an order book

A market overview does not establish full depth or executable conditions. Each data level has its own evidence boundary.

Research outcome: Hybrid / Adaptive acquisition

Rather than continuously subscribing every market to the most expensive data layer, the researched approach uses lower-cost observation to identify relevant markets and deeper verification for selected candidates.

Evidence level matters more than an API name

The research distinguishes official documentation, historical observed data, bandwidth estimates and code-level validation. Older documentation helps explain an interface, but current venue behavior still requires fresh verification.

Important boundary

A dispositioned 374-question catalogue does not mean 374 implemented capabilities or trading permissions. Documentation, wire evidence, code wiring and field qualification are different levels.
