DESIGN & RISK PRINCIPLES

Principles that prevent imaginary profit

In a financial system, a fast but incorrect output is more dangerous than an inconclusive one. Much of ArbiFlow's design is shaped around preserving these distinctions.

FAIL-CLOSEDPROVENANCENO FALSE PROFIT

Distinctions that must not collapse

Price Gap≠Executable EdgeA price discrepancy is not necessarily executable.
Request Sent≠Accepted / FilledSubmission is not acceptance or a fill.
Timeout≠RejectionAn unknown response is not a confirmed rejection.
Cancel Requested≠CanceledA cancel request does not close an order.
Market Data≠Market TruthRaw data must be validated.
Shadow Cashflow≠Realized PnLSimulation is not realized profit.

Fail-closed when evidence is insufficient

If market, account, fee or order-rule state is unknown, the system should not fill the gap with assumptions. This may reduce the number of actionable opportunities, but it prevents uncertainty from becoming a financial decision.

NO_ACTION is a valid outcome.

In this project, taking no action when required evidence is incomplete is not a failure of opportunity discovery; it is part of risk control.

Capital truth

  • Each venue and asset balance must be considered separately.
  • Locked or unresolved funds must not be treated as spendable.
  • Manual deposits or transfers must not be represented as fictional destination inventory.
  • Paper and Real must remain separate in balance and fill provenance.

Evidence truth

Every claim belongs to an evidence level: official documentation, historical observation, offline test, field observation, account qualification and finally operator authorization. Passing one level does not automatically pass the next.
